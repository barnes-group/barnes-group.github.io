Product overview

The pressure you need, without a sensor in the mold.

Cavity pressure tells you how stable your injection molding process is. Until now you needed a sensor in the cavity to see it. IntelliGate p measures it behind the valve pin instead.

The melt presses on the valve pin, and the pin presses on a piezoelectric sensor in the pin holder. The signal follows the pressure curve in the cavity, so you can monitor every shot and spot changes in your process.

The sensor is always at the same place, right at the gate. It does not depend on the space in your mold, so every cavity is measured in the same way. It also measures the forces on the valve pin when the gate opens and closes, which a sensor in the cavity cannot see.

IntelliGate p comes from the combination of Männer hot runner know-how and PRIAMUS sensor and process monitoring know-how, both part of SPECTRIX. The concept is patented.

Mold makers

No extra space, machining or wiring in the mold for a cavity sensor, and no responsibility for the sensor installation. You get the hot runner and the sensor from one source.

Molders and process engineers

Monitor every shot, set up your process faster and see changes early. The same sensor position in every cavity makes the curves easy to compare.

Surface and medical parts

Nothing touches the melt in the cavity, so there is no sensor mark on visible surfaces. Made for small and medium parts in multi-cavity molds, for example packaging and medical disposables.

Features

What you get with IntelliGate p.

Six things that change how you measure pressure in your mold. Pick one to see how it works. The charts are measurements from a test mold.

Pressure behind the pin

The cavity pressure, measured at the valve pin.

Instead of measuring in the cavity, IntelliGate p measures the force that the melt puts on the valve pin. The injection phase shows the same curve as a cavity pressure sensor.

  1. The melt pushes on the valve pin. This happens at the gate, at the start of every cavity.
  2. The pin presses on the sensor in the pin holder. A piezoelectric sensor turns the force into an electrical signal.
  3. The signal is converted to pressure. The sensor is calibrated in pC/N and converted to bar with the diameter of the pin.
  4. You see the pressure curve of every shot in your monitoring system.
  • Same curve in the injection phase
  • Same position in every cavity
  • Independent of the mold design
No sensor in the mold

Less work for the mold maker, a clean part for you.

A cavity sensor needs space, a bore, a thread and a wire channel in the mold. It also touches the melt. IntelliGate p avoids all of this.

  1. No extra machining. No sensor bore in the mold insert and no wire channel through the mold.
  2. No imprint on the part. Nothing touches the melt inside the cavity, so there is one contact point less in the cavity.
  3. No dependency on the sensor position. With a cavity sensor the curve depends on where it sits. Here the sensor is always at the gate.
  4. One supplier. The mold maker gets the hot runner and the sensor as one system and does not carry the responsibility for a separate sensor.
Good to know: IntelliGate p measures the cavity pressure equivalent behind the valve pin. The sensor has no flat contact surface, and the friction of the valve guide has an effect on the signal. This is what lets you see the condition of the guide.
  • No sensor bore
  • No sensor imprint
  • Fewer contact points in the cavity
Process monitoring

See your process change, shot by shot.

The curve reacts to the settings of your machine. In tests on a two-cavity test mold with PP, the changes in the process were clearly visible.

  1. Packing pressure. A higher packing pressure gives a higher pressure level after the filling phase.
  2. Injection speed. A higher speed shortens the filling time and moves the closing of the valve pin along the time axis.
  3. Melt temperature. A hotter melt is less viscous, so the pressure that is needed to open the valve pin gets lower.

Test mold with two cylindrical gates of 1.5 mm, card-shaped part of 70 × 50 mm, wall thickness 1 mm, polypropylene.

  • Real-time monitoring
  • Detect good parts
  • Basis for predictive maintenance
Hot runner condition

Also shows what a cavity sensor cannot see.

Because the sensor sits behind the valve pin, it sees forces that never reach the cavity. This gives you information about the hot runner itself.

  1. Opening and closing forces. The sensor also records the pull forces on the valve pin when the gate opens and when the part is released.
  2. Wear of the valve guide. In a test with a damaged guide bushing, the pressure on the pin was clearly higher than with a standard bushing, especially at operating temperature.
  3. Thermal load. The effect grows with temperature, because the pin expands and the manifold moves sideways.

Test with a clean hot runner without melt. Pressure difference between a standard and a damaged guide bushing: about 1 bar (14 psi) at 25 °C (77 °F) and about 8.7 bar (126 psi) at 200 °C (392 °F).

  • Pull forces on the pin
  • Guide wear visible
  • Prepares predictive maintenance
Retrofit

Add it to a hot runner you already have.

You can fit IntelliGate p to existing Männer hot runner systems without a sensor bore in the mold. Pin and cable are removable, so installation and maintenance stay simple.

  1. Replace the actuator parts. You need a new piston, a new piston cover and the new valve pin with sensor. The fixing of the pin is the same as for a standard pin.
  2. Fit a higher insulating plate. The new insulating plate gives the 8 to 10 mm of extra height that the sensor needs.
  3. Connect the cable. Plug the SMP connector into the sensor and lead the cable out of the mold.
  4. Connect the amplifier. Use the Fischer Triax connector. You do not need a new pneumatic plate if the hot runner already uses PS 48.2.
  • Standard pin fixing
  • Removable cable and pin
  • No new pneumatic plate with PS 48.2
Open system

Use it with the equipment you have.

The sensor signal is open. You are not tied to one amplifier or one control system.

  1. Use PRIAMUS equipment. The sensor works with PRIAMUS FillControl and the PRIAMUS amplifiers.
  2. Or use another amplifier. The cable has an SMP connector on the sensor side and a Fischer connector on the amplifier side, so other charge amplifiers can be connected.
  3. Go from monitoring to control. The signal can be used for visualization, for process monitoring and for automatic process control.
  • Open sensor signal
  • Works with PRIAMUS FillControl
  • Third-party amplifiers possible

Hardware

Built into the actuator, not into the mold.

IntelliGate p is part of the pneumatic actuator of a Männer hot runner. It needs a little more height behind the valve pin, and nothing else in the mold changes.

What you need to know before you order.

The sensor sits inside the pin holder. The hot runner needs the PS 48.2 pneumatic actuator and a little more height behind the pin.

  1. New hot runner. Use the standard pneumatic cut-out and a pneumatic or insulating plate that is 12 or 18 mm higher than standard. Or let the pneumatics sink into the plate to keep the standard height.
  2. Retrofit. Replace piston, piston cover and pin, and add a new insulating plate. The extra height is 8 to 10 mm.
  3. Plan the space. 18 mm are needed behind the valve pin, including 6 mm for the connector, with a stroke of 12 mm.

In the assembly box

The tools for the assembly come in a blue assembly box. The installation is the same as for a standard valve pin: the pin is fixed with the standard fixing unit and a left-hand thread.

Removable pin and cable

The standard pin can be removed and exchanged for maintenance. The cable is detachable as well, so you do not need to take the whole actuator apart.

Cable exit in a safe place

The sensor connection is in a non-critical area of the mold, away from outgassing. This protects the connection and keeps the signal clean.

Made for multi-cavity molds

The concept is made for small and medium parts in high volumes, for example in packaging and in medical disposables.
